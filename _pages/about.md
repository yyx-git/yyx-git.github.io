I'm Yaxuan Yang, a Master's student in the Department of Mechanics at Sichuan University. 
My background in mechanics shapes how I approach atmospheric science — I tend to look for the underlying physical mechanisms first, and I bring the tools of fluid dynamics and numerical modeling to problems in tropical cyclone research.

My research lies at the intersection of tropical cyclone dynamics, air–sea–land interaction, and geophysical fluid dynamics. I am particularly interested in how tropical cyclones interact with the Earth system — from landfall processes and Earth's rotation to the possibility of tropical cyclone control through physics-guided AI, using approaches such as reinforcement learning and machine learning.

I am currently working on two related topics centered on landfalling tropical cyclones:

- **Landfalling tropical cyclones perturb Earth's rotation**
- **Reinforcement learning for pre-landfall typhoon intensity control**

As a researcher, I am drawn to questions that seem counterintuitive at first but turn out to reveal something physically meaningful. I value curiosity and the courage to break new ground. I care deeply about physical understanding, yet I try to stay open to the unknown. Above all, I hope my research can be vivid and engaging — offering people a fresh way of seeing tropical cyclones, and contributing to both fundamental science and disaster mitigation.

### Research interests

- Tropical cyclone dynamics and landfall processes
- Air–sea–land interaction
- Earth's rotation and atmosphere–solid Earth coupling
- Reinforcement learning for tropical cyclone control

### Methods

- Numerical modeling with CM1
- Reanalysis-based diagnosis
- Data analysis with MATLAB and Python
- Reinforcement learning
